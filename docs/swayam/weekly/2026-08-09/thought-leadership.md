# Thought Leadership — 2026-08-09

**Title:** The Five-Minute Cliff — Where Click-to-WhatsApp Intent Goes to Die

---

Your Meta ad did its job. A parent tapped. WhatsApp opened. They are ready to talk.

Then silence — or a reply that arrives tomorrow morning with a fee PDF attached.

This is the **five-minute cliff**. Not a marketing theory. A conversion pattern repeated across Indian coaching institutes, schools, and EdTech admissions teams running Click-to-WhatsApp campaigns.

**The numbers operators cite:**
- Reply within **5 minutes** → up to **8× higher** conversion vs one-hour delay
- **60%+ of enquiries** arrive outside office hours
- CTWA best practice: first message answered in **under 30 seconds** (often automated)

**Why it hurts more on CTWA than lead forms:**
The parent is already in chat. They are comparing you live against competitors who replied first. A slow or generic greeting wastes the peak-intent moment your ad purchased.

Meta knows this. Slow first-response patterns on CTWA can degrade delivery quality — you pay for clicks, then train the algorithm you cannot close the loop.

**The operational mistake:**
Teams celebrate chat volume and CPL while ignoring **time-to-first-reply**. Marketing owns the ad. Counsellors own the conversation. Nobody owns the five minutes in between.

**What actually works:**
1. Instant automated acknowledgement with course context from the ad
2. One qualifying question — not a brochure dump
3. Human counsellor handoff with full thread history within the hour
4. After-hours coverage so 9 PM enquiries don't wait until 10 AM

**The reframe:**
You do not have a lead quality problem. You have a **speed-to-first-reply** problem disguised as a creative fatigue problem.

Measure the cliff before you increase ad spend.
